## About page

> ### About The Board Tech Index
>
> Technology reached the boardroom as a hazard. Regulation put cyber risk on the agenda, and boards responded almost universally. But the reason technology matters to a company is mostly upside: what it lets the firm build, enter, automate and pre-empt.
>
> The Board Tech Index asks whether boards have taken ownership of that upside, or only of the downside.
>
> We answer it from the public record. A board that owns something has to say which body owns it, so rather than ask directors what they believe they govern, we read what their companies have committed to in writing. Our benchmark scores 20 technology topics across six domains for 446 large-cap U.S. companies, and every score traces to a quoted sentence in a 10-K, proxy statement or committee charter.
>
> The first edition found that 99.8% of boards own technology risk, while 9.9% own any part of their innovation portfolio. 94% oversee technology risk more closely than technology value.
>
> **What you'll get**
>
> - **The benchmark, updated.** New proxy seasons, new charters, and how the numbers move.
> - **Deep dives.** One domain, sector or topic at a time: AI, venture and ecosystem, digital transformation, committee design.
> - **Method notes.** What we measure, what we can't, and which way each limit pushes the numbers.
>
> **Who it's for**
>
> Directors, general counsel and corporate secretaries, governance and IR teams, investors and stewardship teams, and anyone who advises boards on technology.
>
> **What we don't do**
>
> We don't publish individual company scores until they've cleared human review, and we don't treat disclosure as a verdict on governance quality. Every figure is a floor: a board can govern technology well and not say so.

## First post outline

**Working title:** Boards Own Technology's Risk. Almost None Own Its Upside.

**Subtitle:** What 446 board filings say about who governs technology as an opportunity.

**Length:** about 1,500–2,000 words, 3 charts.

1. **Open with the contrast (150 words).** Cyber is the success story: 99.8% of boards own it, 75% with a named owner and stated cadence. That happened because regulation required it. Then pivot: nothing required boards to own the upside, and the filings show it.
2. **The question and why filings can answer it (200 words).** A board that owns something has to say which body owns it. Introduce documented ownership and the 0–3 scale in plain language.
3. **Finding 1: the oversight cliff (300 words, chart).** Domain ownership bars from the site: technology risk 99.8%, emerging technologies 46.4%, AI 29.1%, corporate venture 15.0%, digital transformation 14.8%, innovation portfolio 9.9%. The 94% positive-gap figure, and why a within-company comparison rules out "they just disclose less".
4. **Finding 2: AI, contained rather than won with (300 words, chart).** 27.4% AI governance vs 2.9% competitive advantage vs 1.3% productivity. Only 2.7% show AI oversight exercised.
5. **Finding 3: committees exist, mandates don't follow (300 words, chart).** 45% of the Fortune 500 have a tech committee with no size gradient (38–51%). 65% of them still route cyber to Audit. Two readings: a strategy body by design, or a committee without the mandate.
6. **What would change these numbers (250 words).** Every figure is a floor. Charters more than double the value score (0.46 vs 0.21) and only 17% were available. Inter-rater reliability is still outstanding. The structure comparison isn't causal.
7. **What a board could do on Monday (200 words).** Name the body that owns technology value, not just risk. Publish the committee charter. Put a decision, not a briefing, on the agenda: an investment, a portfolio call, an M&A screen.
8. **Close and what's next (100 words).** Next posts: a sector cut, then a deep dive on AI. Invite replies from directors.

**Charts to export from the site:** the oversight-cliff bar chart (Findings), the AI topic breakdown (Findings, 20-topic view), and cyber routing among adopters (Committee landscape).

**Check before publishing:** run every figure against [fact-sheet.md](fact-sheet.md), and confirm no company is named alongside a score.
